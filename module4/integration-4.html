Putting It Together: One Patient, Three Tools

You've practiced the downward arrow, the self-perpetuating loop, and the thought record as separate skills. A clinician uses them as one motion. Follow a single patient through the sequence: the arrow finds the core belief, the loop shows how that belief keeps itself alive, and the thought record becomes the tool she takes home to interrupt it. The last stage shows what that looks like on an ordinary day, months later, with no therapist in the room.

R
Meet Renata. She started a new job three months ago and came to therapy because she feels a constant, low-grade dread about her work. She rereads her emails many times before sending, avoids asking questions, and has started hiding unfinished work from her manager. When her manager gives neutral or even positive feedback, she hears criticism. She's exhausted, and she's convinced she's about to be found out.
