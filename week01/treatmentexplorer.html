Behavioral Activation

Mechanism dominant
Major Depressive Disorder Adult Deep Dive Available
Self-monitoring of daily activities with mood ratings, functional analysis of avoidance patterns, values-based activity scheduling, and graded task assignment that reintroduces avoided activities in small, achievable steps. Structured around identifying triggers and avoidance responses, then planning alternative approach behaviors regardless of momentary motivation.
Increased activation and reduced avoidance restore contact with positive reinforcement in the environment, reversing the withdrawal-and-low-reward cycle that maintains depressed mood. Increased reinforcement, reduced avoidance, increased reward engagement.
Engages the mesolimbic reward circuit. Treatment is associated with changes in reward-related activation of the ventral striatum and nucleus accumbens, regions that show blunted reward responsiveness in depression. Reward-circuit account; the causal chain from activation to reward-signal change is still an active research question.
Well suited to low motivation and to more severe depression, since it asks clients to act before motivation returns rather than after. The straightforward, concrete structure makes it comparatively easy to deliver and to scale. Designated a well-established treatment for depression by the American Psychological Association.

Clinical Note: Best for low motivation.

CBT (cognitive-behavioral therapy) for Depression

Mechanism dominant
Major Depressive Disorder Adult Deep Dive Available
Cognitive restructuring through thought records and Socratic questioning, behavioral experiments that test the accuracy of predictions, and an integrated behavioral component of activity scheduling, all supported by between-session homework. Collaborative empiricism: each automatic thought is treated as a hypothesis to examine against evidence rather than a belief to argue down.
Correcting biased information processing and reappraising maladaptive automatic thoughts reduces the negative cognitive bias that maintains depression, while an integrated behavioral component reduces avoidance and rebuilds reward engagement. Reduced avoidance, increased reward engagement, cognitive reappraisal.
Associated with diminished limbic (amygdala) reactivity to negative stimuli and altered prefrontal and cingulate activity, consistent with strengthened top-down prefrontal regulation over emotional responses. Subgenual anterior cingulate change tracks symptom improvement. Prefrontal–limbic regulation account, drawn from a systematic review of task-based fMRI studies.
Benefits from a client's capacity to engage with and practice cognitive skills, a structured collaborative alliance, and consistent completion of between-session homework. Explicit case formulation links each technique to the individual client. Structured, skills-based, and homework-dependent by design.

Clinical Note: Use PHQ-9; start with BA.
